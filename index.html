Frequently Asked Questions

What services does Southline Development provide?

We specialize in three areas: (1) Building permit expediting for Chicago and suburban construction projects, (2) Data center site development and feasibility studies, and (3) Battery storage and energy infrastructure permitting under Illinois's Clean Grid Act (CRGA). All services include full municipal permitting, utility coordination, and project management support.

What areas do you serve?

We serve Chicago, all Chicagoland suburbs (including Evanston, Oak Park, Naperville, Elk Grove Village, Aurora, Joliet, and Schaumburg), and projects throughout Illinois and the Midwest. Our deep relationships with Chicago DOB, DPD, CDOT, and suburban building departments enable faster approvals across the region.

How much do your services cost?

Pricing varies by project complexity: Building permit expediting ranges from $500-$5,000 per project. Data center feasibility studies start at $5,000. Battery storage permitting ranges from $8,000-$30,000. We provide transparent, upfront pricing with no hidden fees. Contact us for a detailed quote based on your specific project.

How long does the permitting process take?

With our expediting services, most Chicago building permits are pulled in 2-4 weeks compared to 6-12 weeks for DIY applications. Data center and energy storage projects typically take 3-6 months from feasibility through final approvals, depending on municipal complexity and utility coordination requirements.

Do you work with Chicago Department of Buildings (DOB)?

Yes, we have extensive experience and established relationships with Chicago DOB, Department of Planning & Development (DPD), and CDOT. We handle all application submissions, plan reviews, corrections, and inspections on your behalf, navigating the bureaucracy so you don't have to.

Can you help with Illinois battery storage projects?

Absolutely. We specialize in navigating the Illinois Clean and Reliable Grid Affordability Act (CRGA) requirements, including ComEd and Ameren interconnection approvals, municipal special use permits, fire marshal coordination (NFPA 855), and IEPA environmental compliance. Illinois is targeting 3,000 MW of storage by 2030, creating significant opportunity.
